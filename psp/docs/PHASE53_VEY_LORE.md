# Phase 53 Vey lore foundation

This is the internal lore reference for Emberwake. It defines the setting's
rules so later dialogue, environments, encounters, and story scenes stay
consistent. It is not player-facing exposition.

## Vey

Vey is a living current that moves through soil, water, weather, and the
memory of places. It is not fuel that people manufacture or own. In healthy
land it settles into repeating local patterns: the warmth around volcanic
stone, the pull of a tidal channel, the hush of a deep wood, or the charge
before a storm. When those patterns are disturbed, the land and its creatures
show the strain.

## Veylings and ordinary animals

Veylings are animals whose bodies can sense, hold, and answer a local Vey
pattern. Their unusual techniques are physical expressions of that bond:
heat, currents, rooted growth, static, stone, wind, frost, shadow, and toxin
are all ways a creature's body has learned to answer its environment.

Ordinary animals share the same world but do not form a stable Vey bond. They
are never lesser creatures or failed Veylings. They fill normal ecological
roles, and many people study their behavior to understand changes in a
region's Vey.

## History and relationships

People have known Veylings for as long as settlements have kept local records.
The first trail markers, weather shelters, and spring shrines were practical
agreements with places where Veylings gathered. Modern keepers continue that
tradition by observing habitats, helping injured Veylings, and traveling only
when a mutual bond forms.

A bond is built through care, familiarity, and consent. A partner may refuse
a command, flee danger, or stay with its habitat. Vey vessels and field tools
are safe travel equipment, not ownership marks. Capturing a Veyling represents
offering a protected traveling shelter; the game should present release and
rest as valid outcomes.

## Growth, evolution, and battle

As a Veyling gains experience, its body learns to carry a wider and steadier
Vey pattern. Evolution is a gradual threshold in that learning, not a sudden
replacement of identity. It is affected by the creature's own growth, health,
and relationship to its surroundings; the current level system is the
gameplay-facing simplification of that process.

Veylings test one another because controlled displays establish distance,
territory, trust, and capability without the harm of a real territorial fight.
Human-guided battles use agreed trail rules: stop when a partner can no longer
continue, restore it afterward, and never treat defeat as a reason to discard
it. Guardians use formal challenges to decide whether a traveler understands
the responsibilities of entering a sensitive route.

## Ecology

Different habitats foster different Vey patterns, so local Veylings are tied
to environments without being imprisoned by an element label. Fernveil's old
canopy favors Grove, Wind, and Toxin expressions. Sunthread's water and storm
channels foster Tide, Spark, and Frost forms. Hollowstone's mineral darkness
supports Ember, Stone, and deep-water forms. Glasswake will mix patterns
unstably because its currents have been altered.

This gives each region an ecological identity and gives the main story a clear
stake: a damaged current changes habitats, not merely battle difficulty.

## Writing guardrails

- Explain Vey through behavior, landmarks, keeper customs, and small dialogue
  moments before using direct definitions.
- Keep Veylings as animals with agency and needs, never collectible equipment.
- Avoid a single universal authority that ranks every Veyling or territory.
- Let different communities describe Vey differently while preserving the
  shared physical rules in this document.
