# Phase 56 protagonist identity

## The player character

The player character is an older teenager, roughly **16–18**, raised in
**Hearth Clearing**. Their name, appearance, pronouns, voice, and exact family
details remain player-defined. The existing sprite represents a practical
trail outfit, not a fixed named hero.

## Background

The player grew up around Wayfarer Lodge and knows the ordinary work that keeps
Hearth connected: carrying messages, keeping paths clear, noticing weather,
and giving resting Veylings space. They are familiar with Veylings as neighbors
of the settlement, but they have never been responsible for traveling with one
until the opening of the game.

Their strength is attentiveness, not an ambition to prove superiority. The
player notices small changes—a silent marker, a disturbed nest, a stream
running the wrong way—and takes those signs seriously when others are busy
with immediate local needs.

## Relationship with Veylings

The protagonist begins with respectful familiarity rather than expert skill.
Their starter partnership is the first time they must make decisions for a
Veyling while away from home. The journey teaches care, observation, and
cooperation: a partner is never a tool, and a route is never safe simply
because it was safe yesterday.

This supports gameplay directly. Capturing, healing, release, battle choices,
and guardian challenges all express how the player learns to travel
responsibly with Veylings.

## Personal motivation and reason for travel

Hearth's fading trail markers matter personally because the player has relied
on those paths their whole life. When the spring goes quiet and a familiar
local Veyling bolts east, the problem is no longer distant news. The player
wants to understand what changed and protect a home that has always protected
them.

Mira's request to take observations to Fernveil gives the player a concrete
first reason to leave. As the Quieting connects more regions, that errand
grows into a commitment: return useful knowledge to the people affected and
help the route recover instead of merely passing through it.

## Connections to the recurring cast

## Player-identification rules

- Do not assign the player a voiced personality, fixed gender, or predefined
  rivalry.
- Dialogue choices can express care, curiosity, caution, or resolve without
  changing the main plot.
- The player earns trust by what they observe and how they treat partners, not
  by becoming an institutional champion.
- Home remains emotionally present through return visits, save points, and
  messages from Hearth rather than through a fixed family history.
